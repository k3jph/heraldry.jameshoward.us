## A defined cross in a defined shield

The consulted state-symbol law describes a silver double cross on the central raised part of a blue triple mount in a red early-Gothic shield. It specifies the treatment of the cross ends and rounded mounts, and includes digital representation within its scope.[^sk-state] These details make the device more precise than an instruction to draw any white cross on three blue hills.

The description supplies a formal model readers can operate. Identify shield, field, mount and cross, then inspect their relationships. The double cross is a particular charge structure; the mount supplies its position and support. Changing the ends of the arms or the relative arrangement can matter even where the overall silhouette remains recognizable.

Slovakia therefore needs its own chapter beside [Czechia](/traditions/czechia/) and [Hungary](/traditions/hungary/). Historical overlap and related figures provide comparison opportunities, but the present state device and its public administration have their own dated instruments.

## Color and material are not identical

The law permits white as a representation of the cross’s silver and addresses non-colored or material realizations.[^sk-state] A stone carving or monochrome reproduction can therefore be investigated without inferring that the design has lost its prescribed tinctures.

The distinction is between armorial description and physical realization. Argent names a relationship in the design; white paint is one way of making it visible. Metal, stone and a screen can realize that relationship through different technologies. A photograph of a stone cross does not independently establish that the underlying device was intended to be heraldically gray.

For a worked catalogue, record the design’s tincture separately from the object’s material and surface appearance. That permits both heraldic and conservation research. Combining all three into one “color” field would obscure whether a difference arose through description, manufacture, weathering or photography.

## The register searches more than municipalities

The official Heraldic Register interface distinguishes legal persons from natural persons and offers name and administrative filters.[^sk-register] Its categories show that the public collection cannot be accurately described as only a list of town shields.

A legal person can be a corporate or institutional bearer; a natural person is an individual. The identity of the holder matters to the interpretation of the record. A design recorded for one organization does not become a personal entitlement for its employees, and an individual entry does not identify every person sharing the surname.

A search result should therefore retain holder type, record reference and the consulted description. Name matching is a discovery step, not a completed identification. The researcher still needs to connect the result with the person or body under investigation through documentary evidence.

## The public interface is a selected record

IVES describes separate editorial and public components of HERALDREG. The public side presents selected data, including blazon, authors and a record reference, together with images.[^sk-application] A public entry is therefore a view into an administrative dataset rather than necessarily the complete file.

This matters when a field is absent. The public page’s silence about a document does not prove that the document never existed. Conversely, the system’s ability to store a category does not prove that every particular record contains it. Distinguish interface design, an individual entry’s data and the underlying administrative file.

The named author field supplies an especially useful research opportunity. A design has makers as well as bearers. Comparing creators across a dated corpus can reveal artistic and institutional networks, provided the researcher first checks what the field records and avoids assuming that every credited author performed the same role.

## The state sign and state achievement differ

The consulted law also distinguishes the sign of the state arms from the full shield arrangement.[^sk-state] That distinction resembles the difference between state arms and derived public graphic forms in [Estonia](/traditions/estonia/), while the exact local rules remain specific.

A device extracted from a shield can retain recognizable charges yet operate as another prescribed public form. Record the extraction and its governing use rather than automatically calling it a defective coat of arms. The legal instrument determines whether the form is recognized and how it may be represented.

Further research should connect municipal seals and historical arms with modern local adoption records. A revival may preserve an old figure while selecting new tinctures or simplifying its arrangement. That process requires an object-and-act chronology, not a blanket assumption that every modern municipal device has remained unchanged since the Middle Ages.

## Workshop: preserve the public data’s scope

A fictional researcher downloads register images, discards holder type and authors, and labels every file “Slovak town arms.” Explain what the export has lost. Propose fields that would allow later study of municipalities, other organizations, individuals and design makers without conflating them.

<details class="research-answer"><summary>Discuss the record structure</summary><p>The export loses the distinction between legal and natural persons, the institutional identity of each bearer and the credited creative work. Retain holder category, name, record reference, blazon, authors, image provenance and access date. Where the public interface omits the underlying act, record that limitation and seek the file rather than inventing an adoption history.</p></details>

For graduate work, select ten municipal entries and compare their modern descriptions with earlier seals or armorial witnesses. Identify which relationships are directly documented and which are reconstructed. The study should explain a defined set of local revivals or continuities, not extrapolate from a few attractive images to every Slovak armorial practice.
