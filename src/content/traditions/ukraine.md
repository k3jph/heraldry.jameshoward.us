## A separate history with intersecting jurisdictions

Ukraine belongs in its own chapter. Historical overlap with Lithuania, Poland, the Habsburg lands or the Russian Empire explains particular records and administrative settings; it does not turn the Ukrainian tradition into an appendix of any one of them. The useful unit is a dated bearer within a dated jurisdiction, followed across changes where the evidence permits.

The Internet Encyclopedia of Ukraine follows several distinct strands: early Kyivan princely signs, the Galician lion, Cossack armory and the symbols chosen by the republics of 1918.[^ua-arms] These strands supply different kinds of continuity. A territorial lion, a military-community figure and a princely mark are not interchangeable simply because later national histories place them together.

Our model consequently has two timelines. One follows designs and their documented uses. The other follows the institutions governing the relevant territory or bearer. A change on one timeline may affect the other, but the relationship must be demonstrated. This is particularly necessary where an old territorial name covers areas divided among several present administrative units.

## The trident and the chronology of armory

The reference account connects the trident with Volodymyr and his dynasty, while Article 20 of the Constitution identifies the princely sign as the principal element of the great state emblem and as the small state emblem.[^ua-arms][^ua-constitution] A sign can therefore be reused in a modern armorial setting without requiring us to call every tenth-century use a mature Western coat of arms.

Describe the early object in its own terms first: a mark on a coin, seal or other dated witness. Then ask what can be established about its bearer, repeated form and relationship to other dynastic signs. Only afterward should we explain its modern constitutional reuse. That order keeps a national emblem’s present importance from determining the classification of much earlier evidence.

There is a related distinction between continuity of a recognizable motif and continuity of an institution. A modern state may deliberately select a historical sign to express an account of its past. The act of selection is itself important evidence. It does not imply that every political and legal mechanism governing the sign remained unchanged through the intervening centuries.

## Volhynia crosses the border on the page

Andriy Grechylo’s conference summary follows Volhynia from the Ruthenian kingdom into the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, then into the Polish Crown after the Union of Lublin in 1569, and later into the Russian Empire. It identifies the cross on early-fifteenth-century evidence and discusses its modern regional reuse.[^ua-volhynia] That chronology explains the Lithuanian connection while also showing why “Ukraine falls under Lithuania” is too broad.

The same summary distinguishes historical Volhynia from present oblast boundaries.[^ua-volhynia] A modern region can use a form of an older territorial sign while covering only part of the old territory. Several modern regions may refer to the same historical landscape. A database needs historical extent, current administrative extent and the date of each identifying act; one map polygon cannot do all three jobs.

A cross on a seal also does not become self-identifying simply because we recognize it from a modern regional shield. Begin with the seal’s named issuer and documentary attachment. Then compare later explicitly identified representations. Recognition is a hypothesis that the witness can strengthen or weaken, rather than a substitute for reading the record.

## Cossack identification and state seals

The encyclopedia identifies an armed Cossack with a musket as the Hetman state’s emblem and records family-armorial use among the Cossack officer elite.[^ua-arms] These two levels should remain separate. A community or state emblem identifies a corporate bearer; an officer family’s device identifies a lineage. Evidence that both existed does not establish that they followed identical rules of transmission.

The sphragistics account provides a further comparison through the distinct seal arrangements of the 1918 republic and Hetmanate.[^ua-seals] A state can retain or rework a national identifying motif while changing the seal’s inscription, surrounding composition and documentary function. The seal is an instrument in a system of authentication, not merely the arms placed inside a circle.

To study that system, catalogue the matrix, an impression and the attached document as separate witnesses. Record the language of the legend and the issuer’s office. A small damaged impression may preserve documentary authority while losing details needed for visual classification. A modern clean drawing reverses that balance: it can clarify the design while supplying no evidence that a particular historical document was sealed.

## A constitutional requirement is not an enacted picture

Article 20 specifies the procedure and components for establishing a great emblem, including the small emblem and the arms of the Zaporozhian Host, through a law adopted by the required supermajority.[^ua-constitution] The passage tells us how such an emblem is to be established. It does not prove that an image circulating online is the enacted result.

When studying a proposed great emblem, record the proposal’s author, institutional stage and date. A competition entry, a government-supported draft and a promulgated law are different acts. The constitutional text may explain why the proposal combines particular elements, but the proposal’s legal status still requires its own evidence.

This distinction gives readers a practical tool for evaluating online heraldry. A polished rendering is evidence that someone made a polished rendering. Its status follows from the instrument that identifies and adopts it. The better the artwork, the more tempting it is to forget to ask for the instrument.

## A princely sign survives in a different emblematic world

The National Bank of Ukraine identifies Volodymyr’s gold *zlatnyk* and silver *sriblianyk* coinage as early witnesses to the princely sign now known as the trident.[^ua-money-history] The coins bring the discussion down to named classes of objects. They preserve an image alongside figures and inscriptions, rather than offering an isolated modern logo whose original context has disappeared.

The sign’s date is crucial to classification. An early princely mark can participate in dynastic identification without belonging to the mature hereditary shield-and-blazon system familiar from later medieval Western Europe. Calling it a “coat of arms” in a broad modern account can be understandable shorthand, but a detailed history should explain the distinction. The mark’s later adoption as a state coat is another documented act, not a reason to project all later conventions backward.

Read the sign’s geometry before attempting to explain its origin. Record the uprights, curves, junctions and openings in the particular specimen. A comparison of several early signs may reveal variation. It cannot be reduced safely to a list of modern tridents presumed identical because the same English noun describes them.

Interpretations of what the shape originally represents require another evidentiary step. A resemblance to a bird, weapon, letter or object can generate a hypothesis. A convincing historical argument then needs dated comparisons, inscriptions or other evidence. The visual resemblance alone does not compel the identification. This is an especially useful connection with the [tamgas chapter](/traditions/tamgas/), where compact signs likewise require careful attribution and contextual reading.

## Coin, banknote and shield make different kinds of reference

The National Bank’s account connects Heorhii Narbut’s republican banknote design with the trident known from early coinage.[^ua-money-history] The connection is a deliberate modern use of an older identifying sign. A designer can create a claim of historical continuity through selection and placement without reproducing every feature of the original coin.

The change of medium matters. A coin uses a circular metal surface, relief and inscriptions. A banknote uses a rectangular paper composition, typography, denomination and security-related elements. A shield gives the sign a defined heraldic field. The mark may remain recognizable across the three, while its relation to the surrounding information changes.

For a comparative exercise, draw only the available areas: a circle, a banknote rectangle and a shield. Place a simplified trident in each, then add the kinds of information appropriate to that medium. The exercise is hypothetical, not a reconstruction of an undocumented historical specimen. It demonstrates how the same motif can act as one component of several distinct objects.

A numismatic caption should therefore identify the coin, issuer, date range, side and relevant inscription. A banknote caption should identify issue, denomination and designer where documented. A heraldic caption should identify bearer, adopted form and source. “Ancient Ukrainian symbol” is an orientation, not a substitute for those particulars.

## The 1918 republic separated adoption from drawing approval

The Ukrainian Institute of National Memory distinguishes adoption of the trident on **25 February 1918** from approval of Vasyl Krychevskyi’s drawings on **22 March**. Its account identifies the olive-wreath presentation and discusses later Narbut proposals that did not receive final adoption.[^ua-1918-design] The sequence shows why a design history needs verbs as well as dates.

An act can select a sign before the finished graphic model exists. An artist can complete a drawing before an institution approves it. A commission can approve a proposal that never becomes the enacted state emblem. These stages should remain separate in the historical account, especially when several governments or institutions acted within a short political transition.

External ornament changes the presentation too. A trident within a wreath and a trident without that wreath can preserve the principal sign while expressing a different adopted form. Describe the whole composition when comparing them. A cropped image can be useful for studying the sign’s geometry, but it cannot establish whether the original object displayed the surrounding wreath.

The case also demonstrates how public familiarity can precede definitive heraldic adoption. Currency and seals make a sign widely visible. Later acts can formalize its position and drawing. The direction of influence has to be reconstructed from the dated witnesses rather than assumed from whichever object a modern reader encounters first.

## The Cossack with a firearm represents a host

The Institute of National Memory describes the Cossack-with-firearm emblem on seals of hetmans and officers and distinguishes later use by the related Zaporozhian institutions, including an added spear in the lower-host presentation.[^ua-1918-design] The emblem identifies a military-political community. It should not be reduced to a generic picture of an armed individual.

The figure’s clothing, stance and carried weapon matter to identification. A firearm held or carried in a particular way differs from a sword or spear simply substituted because all three signify military activity. Historical witnesses may show variations; describe them from the actual object. A broad subject classification cannot replace the visual evidence needed to compare designs.

The seal’s issuer supplies another distinction. A hetman can use an institutional emblem when acting in office, while personal arms identify another relationship. The image of the Cossack does not automatically represent the officeholder’s portrait, and its occurrence on a seal does not automatically turn it into the officeholder’s hereditary coat. Legends and document context help identify the act being authenticated.

For a source study, compare one documented institutional seal with one documented personal achievement. Record bearer, issuer, legend, date and the design’s position in the object. This makes the distinction operational: a reader should be able to explain what each object identifies without assuming that every emblem used by the same person belongs to the same category.

## Mazepa’s arms invite interpretation through armorial verse

Heorhii Shapovalov and Oleksandr Bielov investigate the cross-anchor interpretation in Ivan Mazepa’s arms using several kinds of evidence, including late-seventeenth- and early-eighteenth-century written sources and armorial verse.[^ua-mazepa-cross-anchor] Their study offers an attributed argument about meaning. It is a useful starting point because it asks how contemporary words and images were related.

Begin with the physical figure in a particular witness. Describe its upright, lower branches and crossing members without yet deciding what every part symbolizes. Then examine the poem or accompanying text. Does it explicitly call the figure an anchor, a cross, or a combination? Does it use the image to praise constancy, faith or another quality? The answer can establish a contemporary interpretation without proving that the same explanation originated the design.

Armorial verse makes the arms active within patronage and literary culture. A shield can be addressed, praised or elaborated into an argument about its bearer. That text becomes evidence for how the emblem was read in a particular setting. It should be dated and attributed rather than detached into a timeless symbolism dictionary.

This also creates a useful comparison with named Polish coats and Ukrainian princely signs. A figure can have a formal armorial identity, an older visual history and a contemporary literary interpretation. Those layers may support one another, but they are not interchangeable. The historian’s account becomes richer when it can keep all three visible.

## Lviv’s civic lion has to remain inside its gateway

Andrii Grechylo’s research account traces the lion-in-gateway design and its restoration in **1990**. The adopted civic composition places a gold walking lion in a gold gateway with three towers on blue; the central tower is taller, and the gate is open.[^ua-lviv-grechylo] This is a specific city design, not simply any Galician lion placed on a shield.

The gateway defines the lion’s spatial setting. A reconstruction should leave the opening large enough for the animal to be clearly within it. If the lion is placed below the gateway, the same two motifs have acquired another relationship. If the doorway is closed by a visible gate, the artist has changed an explicitly described feature. These are structural questions before they are stylistic ones.

The lion’s posture is equally useful. A walking lion differs from a rampant one. Compare that difference with the territorial lion traditions discussed earlier in this chapter. Related historical subjects can appear in different compositions because they identify different bearers or different moments in a design’s history. A recognizable animal should initiate comparison, rather than conclude it.

The larger civic achievement adds external figures and ornaments.[^ua-lviv-grechylo] Read them separately from the gate and lion inside the shield. A complete city presentation can make additional historical references without inserting them as extra charges into the principal field. This preserves both a relatively compact shield and a richer ceremonial arrangement.

## The modern small arms have a dated legal model

The parliamentary resolution of **19 February 1992**, number **2137-XII**, adopts the trident as the small state arms and identifies it as the principal element of a future great coat. It refers to attached colour and schematic representations.[^ua-1992-resolution] The act supplies a legal witness for the modern state form. It is distinct from the ancient coin sign and the 1918 republican presentation, even though it deliberately refers to the same historical emblematic lineage.

The paired drawings serve complementary purposes. A colour model establishes the tinctural presentation; a schematic model clarifies construction without depending on pigment. Compare both before reconstructing a small digital image. The emblem’s openings and relative line widths contribute to recognition, particularly when the shape is reduced or reproduced in relief.

A monochrome rendering also needs a clearly stated purpose. It may faithfully reproduce the geometry while omitting tinctures. That omission is acceptable in many forms of analysis or material production, but a caption should not pretend the colours are visible when they are supplied by the adopted model. The source tells us which information comes from the specimen and which comes from the specification.

This distinction helps explain how a highly recognizable sign can remain flexible across media without becoming arbitrary. The principal identity is stable enough to appear on a coin, document, building or flag. A particular official use can still impose a more exact model, proportion or colour requirement.

## A signed first-reading resolution is not the finished great-arms law

The parliamentary record for bill **5712**, registered in **2021**, records adoption in principle on **24 August 2021** and preparation for a second reading. The linked resolution **1707-IX** instructs the committee to revise the bill for that stage.[^ua-great-bill][^ua-great-first-reading] At the October 2026 source check, that bill record was still procedural evidence for a proposal. It should not be presented as final enactment of the illustrated great arms.

The distinction matters because a procedural resolution is itself an adopted and signed act. A reader who sees that status without checking the act’s subject can conclude that the substantive emblem was enacted. The resolution’s actual work is to advance a bill through the legislative process. Its existence does not convert the bill’s proposed design into the final law.

Compare three records: the Constitution’s requirement, the bill’s proposed composition, and the act recording a stage of its consideration. Each establishes something real. None can silently substitute for the final enactment that would be required to make the proposed image the adopted great coat. The image’s official publication as a proposal should remain visible in its caption.

This is an especially clear graduate exercise in reading administrative evidence. A government source can be entirely authentic and still establish a narrower claim than the researcher wants. Careful work does not merely cite an official URL; it identifies which act the URL contains and what that act actually does.

## Graduate workshop: give each Ukrainian witness its own question

Build a chronological dossier with one princely coin, one 1918 design or seal, one documented Cossack institutional emblem, one personal-armorial interpretation and the 1992 resolution. Add the current bill record as a separate legislative-process witness. Use a column stating the question each item can answer.

Then write a comparison of the early sign and the modern coat. Explain visual continuity, the different emblematic systems in which the witnesses operate, and the documentary acts that establish modern adoption. Add one paragraph distinguishing an enacted symbol from an officially published proposal.

<details class="research-answer"><summary>What should the dossier avoid collapsing?</summary><p>A princely sign is not automatically a mature medieval coat; a Cossack institutional emblem is not automatically a hetman’s personal arms; a literary interpretation is not automatically the design’s origin. The 1992 act establishes a modern small coat, while the 2021 first-reading resolution advances a proposal. The history becomes coherent through those relationships, without requiring Ukraine’s traditions to be filed beneath another country’s chapter.</p></details>

## Workshop: give the dates their jobs

Construct a four-row chronology for a Kyivan princely sign, the Volhynian cross, a 1918 state-seal arrangement and the constitutional provision. For each row, distinguish the historical event from the date of the consulted publication. Then explain where a Lithuanian administrative connection belongs and where it does not.

<details class="research-answer"><summary>Discuss the chronology</summary><p>The Lithuanian connection belongs to a defined period and territory in Volhynia’s history. It does not supply a universal parent category for Kyivan, Cossack or modern Ukrainian identification. The 1918 seal evidence concerns the governments of that period; Article 20 concerns a later constitutional framework. Publication dates identify the research witnesses and should not replace the dates of the events they describe.</p></details>

For graduate work, trace one regional emblem across three administrative settlements. Preserve changing territorial boundaries and identify which later representations are deliberate revivals. The research question should be whether a particular design’s bearer and meaning changed, rather than whether one modern country can retrospectively claim every witness in the sequence.
