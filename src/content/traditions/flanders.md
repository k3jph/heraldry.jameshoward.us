## Private arms in a dated legal framework

An applicant seeking recognition of older arms and an applicant requesting a new design have different things to establish. The Flemish [decree of 3 February 1998](https://codex.vlaanderen.be/Portals/Codex/documenten/1006070.html) gives those differences a statutory form for private persons and institutions. It defines categories of arms, sets conditions for recognition and grants, and establishes an administrative review process. We can therefore ask which claim an application makes, what evidence that claim requires, and which act concludes the procedure.

English-language discussions of Belgian heraldry can be especially misleading when they assume Belgium is a single linguistic and legal unit in every relevant respect. Flemish Community institutions occupy a particular constitutional and territorial context. A serious student should name the Flemish system, identify the scope of the decree, and avoid treating it as automatically governing all Belgian residents or all armorial history in the Low Countries.

The legal text refers to *privé-personen* (natural persons), *instellingen* (institutions), *oud wapen* (old arms) and *nieuw wapen* (new arms). These are legislatively defined categories. Translating *oud* simply as 'ancient' invites a false medieval association. The relevant threshold in the statute is demonstrable public use at least a hundred years earlier, under its specific rules—not an abstract claim of timeless nobility.

## Old arms and new arms have different evidentiary questions

The Flemish law sets distinct procedures for recognizing older arms and granting new ones. For recognition of older personal arms, the applicant must address prior use and documentary descent under the statutory scheme. The law itself contains inheritance language and other conditions that cannot be deduced from the shield's appearance.

For newly granted private arms, the decree defines formal requirements and the persons who may bear the design. It expressly contemplates inheritance terms that may include a female line. This is an especially useful classroom counterexample to the thoughtless assertion that every heraldic inheritance system everywhere excludes women in precisely the same way.

Notice also what a grant does *not* do. The Flemish decree explains that recognition or grant carries the right to bear the arms rather than creating a package of aristocratic privileges. One should separate an armorial identity from noble status and from unrelated social distinctions. The presence of external ornaments can also matter: the law restricts certain crown-like and other elements in newly granted arms.

## The machinery of approval

The corresponding [implementing decision](https://codex.vlaanderen.be/Portals/Codex/documenten/1007545.html) addresses applications, supporting information, consultation, artistic preparation, registration and the final *wapenbrief*. An English-language reader may be tempted to translate the last term as 'letters patent' in every context, but that would smuggle in English constitutional associations. 'Armorial diploma' is safer when explaining the actual Flemish document; the original term should remain visible.

Different participants undertake different functions. The applicant requests recognition or grant. Heraldic advisers examine the proposal and its history. The government has a role in the determination. An artist prepares a rendering. The register preserves a record. The meaning of the whole procedure cannot be reconstructed from a single colour illustration published afterward.

Because the official online text is consolidated, a legal historian must record the version effective on the date relevant to a case. The presence of amendments since 1998 means quoting the current consolidated version as if it were the exact statute in force in 1999 would be a documentary error.

## A graduate documentary problem

Suppose an applicant claims that a nineteenth-century Flemish ancestor used a particular shield, and a contemporary relative seeks official recognition. Identify the documents that would demonstrate prior public use, those establishing the claimant's direct descent, and the records that could show whether the arms were associated with someone else. Distinguish a failure to locate evidence from a finding that an asserted right never existed.

Now suppose an unrelated applicant seeks a newly designed shield with similar charges. Compare the legal path and evidentiary burden. Could a design be rejected for similarity? Which outside ornaments are addressed? Which statutory version governs each imagined filing? Cite actual paragraphs of the Flemish law in Dutch before drawing conclusions.

## The Flemish lion has a history of particular representations

The black lion on gold is immediately recognizable, but a familiar charge does not erase differences among a flag, an armorial shield, and an institutional logo. The Flemish Parliament’s historical dossier follows the lion through an earlier emblem containing a crown and stars and the later correction of the formally specified arms.[^vl-symbols-history] The sequence supplies an actual case in which graphic practice and legal description affected one another.

The heraldic question begins with composition. Is the crown on the lion, above the shield, or part of a surrounding emblem? Are the stars charges within the field or separate graphic elements? A reproduction that retains “a lion, a crown, and five stars” without recording their relations cannot distinguish the relevant versions. The objects are an inventory; their arrangement supplies the meaning that the historical comparison needs.

The institutional question then asks which version belonged to which decision and use. The dossier distinguishes the earlier graphic identity from the corrected specification. A dated letterhead can therefore preserve a real institutional display while differing from the later arms. It is evidence of what was used, rather than a timeless template for every subsequent reproduction. The change needs to be explained through its own history, not dismissed as an artist accidentally adding accessories.

A controlled drawing exercise makes the problem visible. Draw a fictional lion alone on a gold shield, then add stars to the field, then put a crown above the shield. Write three descriptions. The recognizable core survives while the complete compositions differ. The student can now inspect the historical sequence with a vocabulary for distinguishing the shield’s content from its surroundings.

## Lievegem: arms and flag share a vocabulary

The ministerial act of 12 February 2021 confirms Lievegem’s municipal determination after a council decision of 16 December 2020 and heraldic advice of 28 January 2021. It describes the shield as green with a silver wavy fess and a silver chief. The flag has its own specification of four equal-height white and green bands, the third wavy.[^vl-lievegem] Two related symbols are established through the same instrument, while their geometry is described separately.

The shield’s composition can be drawn from a small set of operations. Fill the field green, add a broad silver horizontal band with wavy edges, then place the silver chief at the top. The fess and chief are different ordinaries with different positions. They cannot be exchanged because both are horizontal and share a tincture. Position contributes to identity just as colour does.

The flag solves a different spatial problem. Its equal bands organize a rectangular surface, while the designated wavy band carries a related visual idea. This is an adaptation across media. Stretching the shield image to fill the flag would create another composition, rather than carrying out the flag’s actual description. A palette can establish a visible association without requiring the two layouts to coincide.

The example also shows the value of simplicity. Two tinctures and a few geometric operations produce a recognizable public identity. Simplicity does not make the composition historically empty; the explanatory history belongs in the institution’s documentation and narrative. The design does not need to display every local event as a separate miniature. A reference study can investigate the particular choices while keeping the object legible as a coat of arms.

## Recognition of older arms and creation of new arms

The distinction between *oud wapen* and *nieuw wapen* is a distinction between claims and the evidence required to support them. In the older-arm route, the applicant asserts a prior public use and the relevant connection to its bearer. In the new-arm route, the application concerns a proposed identity and the persons or institution it is to identify. The decree gives these processes different statutory treatments.[^be-vl-decree]

Imagine two invented applications showing exactly the same drawing. Applicant A supplies a located nineteenth-century public witness and a documented ancestral connection. Applicant B provides a modern design rationale and no historical claim. Their pictures look identical, but the applications ask different things of the system. The first needs evaluation of the claimed historical relationship; the second needs evaluation of a new proposal, including its distinctiveness and specified scope.

A third applicant supplies a commercial surname image and calls it ancient. That creates neither the public-use evidence nor the connecting chain needed for the older-arm claim. The problem is not that the drawing looks too modern. A contemporary copy can accurately reproduce an older design; an artificially distressed picture can reproduce an invented one. The evidential relationship, rather than the artwork’s antique appearance, determines what the application can establish.

This is a valuable comparison with [Canada](/traditions/canada/), [Scotland](/traditions/scotland/), and [Germany](/traditions/germany/). Each has its own acts, institutions, and terms. The Flemish distinction should be explained positively on those terms. It does not become more authoritative by being renamed an English grant, and it does not become informal merely because other continental settings organize private arms differently.

## Inheritance belongs to a defined armorial identity

The decree’s provisions for newly granted personal arms contemplate a defined set of bearers and a stated mode of inheritance, potentially including the female line.[^be-vl-decree] The important practical consequence is that the grant’s wording matters. An attractive picture cannot show which persons the act identifies or how the identity is to pass between generations.

For a constructed case, suppose a new coat is granted to a person and to specified descendants. The drawing identifies the design; a family diagram identifies the relationships; the instrument supplies the terms connecting them. A later generation needs all three to understand the claimed continuity. If a relevant surname changes, the genealogical relationship and the armorial terms must be investigated rather than decided by a search box matching letters.

Now imagine a family association adopting a related design. That adds another bearer, rather than automatically making the individual grant identical with the association’s emblem. Membership and descent can overlap, but they are not the same operation. The association’s governing records and the individual’s armorial instrument answer different questions. A careful account can explain the relationship without merging the bearers into an undefined “family.”

Differentiation creates another layer. Related arms may preserve a common core while adding a visible difference. The formal change needs an explanation tied to the actual bearers and instruments. The [Academy’s cadency lesson](/academy/cadency-and-difference/) supplies comparative models; the local record supplies the particular result. A general model becomes useful when it helps us read that record, rather than when it replaces the record.

## A new design needs more than meaningful ingredients

An applicant can begin with a list of meaningful references: a profession, a place, a plant, an inherited story, and a personal value. The heraldic task is to turn those references into one distinguishable composition. Putting five unrelated miniatures in five compartments may preserve the list while failing to create an effective coat of arms. The meanings compete because the figures have little space to become recognizable.

Try an invented design for a maker whose interests include water management and metalworking. One proposal uses a river landscape, a workshop, a tool, a bridge, and a written slogan. Another uses a large tool crossing a wavy ordinary in a limited palette. The second design can associate the activities through form and relationship rather than through an illustrated biography. Its simplicity gives the retained ideas room to function.

The relationship still needs care. A tool lying on a wavy ordinary differs from a tool crossing it. A tool coloured like the background may vanish at small scale unless the arrangement solves the contrast problem. The artist should test the design as a silhouette and in a reduced rendering, then check that the written description preserves what makes it distinct. Meaning and legibility need to be developed together.

This is an original teaching example, not a claim that the imagined design would receive approval. It models a useful part of the work: selecting a few associations, constructing an identifiable relationship, and explaining why the composition earns its details. The actual Flemish procedure then evaluates a real proposal through its own standards and decisions.

## The diploma records a process with several participants

The implementing decision connects the applicant, the Council, the artist, government approval, and the register.[^be-vl-procedure] Each participant contributes something different. The applicant supplies the request and evidence; advice addresses the proposal; the artist realizes the composition; the completed instrument and registered copy preserve the result. A finished illustration can be admired as art while the surrounding process establishes what kind of record it is.

This gives the historian a natural chronology. A preliminary drawing can precede a completed diploma, and a later publication can reproduce the registered result. The dates should not be compressed into one “created” field. The design’s conception, approval, execution, and publication can happen at different times, even when all four events concern one identity.

The 2023 annual report provides a bounded view of actual activity: 16 armorial diplomas, divided into two older-arm recognitions and 14 new grants; four concerned institutions and 12 private persons.[^vl-annual-2023] These categories let the reader see that contemporary practice includes both newly designed and historically grounded identities. The numbers describe that year’s reported acts. They are useful without being inflated into a census of Flemish armigers.

A substantial project could follow one completed case through its available records and compare the published image with the wording of the act. The result would explain how the composition became a recorded identity, including any changes between proposal and completion. That is a fuller account of practice than either a shield gallery or an abstract summary of the statute.

## Workshop: compare a shield, a flag, and an application

First reconstruct Lievegem’s shield and flag from the published descriptions. Keep the two drawings separate and identify exactly which features connect them. Then prepare an invented public identity using a different pair of tinctures and a related geometric operation. Explain whether its flag is an armorial banner or a separately composed adaptation. Similarity of palette does not decide the category.

For the personal-heraldry exercise, prepare two clearly fictional files: an older-arm recognition request and a new-arm proposal. Give each a drawing, bearer, chronology, and statement of what the applicant asks the institution to determine. The historical file needs a located witness and a connecting relationship; the new file needs a composition and defined scope. Comparing the two makes the legal categories operate on real kinds of information rather than remain vocabulary to memorize.
