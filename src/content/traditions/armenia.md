## The four dynasties represented in the quarters

The present Armenian state arms explicitly refer to historical polities represented by the **Artaxiad**, **Arsacid**, **Bagratid**, and **Rubenid** dynasties. The modern quartered shield, supporting eagle and lion, and Mount Ararat with Noah's Ark at the centre assemble these historical references in a new republican composition. The bearers of the historical signs and the state adopting them in **1992** are not the same institution or even the same kind of armiger.

Students comparing quarters should name the four dynasties before turning the composition into a general claim that it “evokes the Armenian past.” The [Armenian government's constitutional symbolism account](https://www.gov.am/en/official/) should be compared with the legislative text and published state model. The formal historic assignment of an emblem to a particular dynasty is a proposition requiring its own medieval or modern historical source, not a consequence of its position in the contemporary shield.

## A modern shield presents a long past

Armenia’s presidential account connects the present arms with the First Republic design by Alexander Tamanian and Hakob Kojoyan and identifies the later 1992 and 2006 legislation.[^am-symbols] The composition therefore has a documented modern history even while its components present an account of much older kingdoms and cultural memory.

That distinction is essential. A modern state can use historical references in an armorial achievement without every component being a surviving unchanged coat of arms from antiquity. The present act of representation and the evidence for an earlier dynasty’s actual device are separate historical questions.

Start with the modern bearer and instrument. Then investigate each historical reference through coins, seals, manuscripts or other dated witnesses. This order permits the national design to make its historical argument while keeping the researcher from confusing that argument with proof of uninterrupted graphic continuity.

## The Constitution establishes the main components

Article 21 names Mount Ararat with Noah’s Ark, the historical kingdoms’ devices, eagle and lion supporters, and objects below the shield. It assigns the detailed description to law.[^am-constitution] The constitutional outline and the more specific design record consequently perform different jobs.

A compositional reading should identify the central landscape, internal divisions, supporters and lower objects before explaining meaning. Those parts participate in different relationships. A central historical-geographical reference is not an ordinary inherited quarter in a family genealogy merely because it appears inside a shield.

For a database, preserve the source’s description of the historical kingdoms as an attributed national representation. Add separate records for the historical witnesses used to investigate those references. That lets readers explore continuity without making the modern official account answer every archaeological or medieval attribution question.

## A royal couple on a silver coin

The Met catalogues a silver tram from the joint rule of Het‘um I and Zabel, dated 1229–52, as loan L.2008.68.1. Its account connects the paired rulers with the marriage through which the Hetumid family gained power and notes a lion with a cross on the reverse.[^am-coin] Here we have an actual medieval object with a defined date range and catalogue identity.

The two sides make different evidence visible. The royal couple concerns representation of authority and relationship. The lion with cross supplies an identifying figure to compare with other dated material. Neither side alone establishes every subsequent armorial description attributed to the dynasty.

This is also a useful example of female authority remaining visible in the material record. A catalogue that records only the lion and calls the coin “the king’s arms” would lose the paired representation that the museum explicitly discusses. The whole object must be catalogued before its identifying motif is extracted for comparison.

## A coin image is not a complete achievement

Coins work within constraints of size, metal, striking and legibility. A figure on a coin can identify a ruler or polity without providing the full shield, crest and supporters of a later heraldic achievement. Classification should follow the actual object rather than adding absent components from a modern illustration.

For a worked comparison, describe the coin’s visible lion independently, then compare it with the modern state account. List similarities and differences without assuming the modern device supplies missing medieval tinctures. A silver coin’s metal is not evidence that every figure impressed on it was heraldically Argent.

The same principle applies to seals. Monochrome production and colored armorial description belong to different representational systems. Color can be established through a related record, but it should not be inferred solely from the material carrying the image. The [tinctures reading](/academy/tinctures/) provides the broader compositional framework.

## Revival and historical attribution have separate dates

The First Republic reference, the later adoption legislation and the medieval coin occupy different points in the chronology.[^am-symbols][^am-coin] A national revival can be historically deliberate while the evidence for an earlier component remains a subject of research. The chapter should state both without allowing either to erase the other.

A useful timeline contains the design’s creation or adaptation, the institutional act establishing it, the publication of a description and each surviving object’s date. A restored or revised drawing may refer back to the First Republic while being manufactured much later. Its modern material date is not a defect; it is another historical fact to preserve.

Compare [Greece](/traditions/greece/) and [Georgia](/traditions/georgia/) through the same model. Each modern composition organizes older references in a local institutional setting. The comparison concerns the mechanisms of selection and representation, rather than a claim that every Christian eagle, lion or saint belongs to one shared coat of arms.

## Museum access does not establish image rights

The coin is on loan, and the catalogue’s availability does not itself establish a reusable photograph licence.[^am-coin] This chapter links the record without reproducing an unverified image. The object’s age and the rights in a contemporary photograph are separate matters.

For an illustrated research project, identify the object owner, photographer and applicable terms. A medieval object may be old enough to have no copyright in its historic design while a modern image or institutional use condition still requires attention. Rights documentation belongs beside the accession and dating, not as an afterthought once a layout has been completed.

## A modern composition selects four royal histories

The present state achievement identifies four royal dynasties through the quartered shield around a central Ararat image. An eagle and lion support the shield; objects below it complete the composition. The presidential account connects the design with Alexander Tamanian and Hakob Kojoyan's work for the First Republic and dates its later statutory adoption.[^am-symbols]

The four dynastic references are representations made by the modern national composition. Their inclusion does not prove that every ancient dynasty used the corresponding shield as a hereditary coat in the later European sense. To investigate the older signs, identify coins, seals, inscriptions or other dated objects from the relevant period. The national achievement organizes historical memory; the older witnesses establish their own forms of identification.

This is a distinction between symbolic ancestry and documentary chronology. A modern state can represent a long history through selected devices without claiming that the complete present achievement existed throughout it. The historian should explain what the selection means to the national design and then examine what the actual ancient or medieval evidence supports.

The external eagle and lion also differ from the animals represented in particular quarters. A supporter stands beside or behind the shield as part of the achievement. A lion on a medieval coin may be an identifying device within the coin's own composition. Similar animals can occupy several levels of a design, each with a different function.

For a first reading, annotate the achievement by layers: central shield, surrounding quarters, supporters and objects below. Then associate each interpretation with its source. This prevents the reader from treating every visible item as a charge in one undivided field and makes the modern design's historical argument easier to follow.

## Ararat combines landscape with a scriptural reference

The central image of Ararat includes Noah's ark in the official account.[^am-symbols] The mountain and vessel connect a recognizable landscape with a scriptural tradition. They should be described as the components of the emblem, rather than treated as archaeological proof of the narrative represented.

The ark is not simply a ship charge that can be moved anywhere in the shield. Its placement on the mountain belongs to the selected scene. A redraw that puts it on a blue wave below the mountain would change the relationship, even if both the mountain and ship remained recognizable. Composition conveys the reference through their position together.

A national emblem can also represent a culturally important landscape beyond the present state's boundary. The presence of a mountain does not, without additional evidence, establish a particular current territorial policy. Its significance in memory, religion and national identity needs to be distinguished from a contemporary legal claim about borders.

For comparison, consider [Slovenia's Triglav](/traditions/slovenia/) and [Georgia's civic mountain motifs](/traditions/georgia/). Each uses a mountain differently: a national graphic figure, a central scene with a scriptural vessel, or part of a civic composition. The common landscape vocabulary does not impose one meaning or one style of depiction on them all.

The colour of the central image should follow the approved Armenian model and statutory description. A modern designer's desire for a naturalistic snow scene is not enough to replace the prescribed rendering. For an educational sketch, distinguish the working diagram from the official artwork so the reader knows what is being simplified.

## Cilicia places Armenian royal imagery in wider networks

The Met's Armenia! exhibition situates the kingdom of Cilicia within relationships involving Latin states, the Catholic Church and Mongol power, alongside Armenian royal and ecclesiastical patronage. Its discussion identifies manuscript production at Hromkla and the work of T'oros Roslin.[^am-cilicia-exhibition] This context gives the chapter's royal coin a setting wider than a self-contained national shield history.

The Mediterranean location matters. Courts, churches and workshops could exchange forms through patronage and political relations. An Armenian manuscript might combine local traditions with motifs familiar from neighbouring cultures. That does not mean every borrowed ornament was a coat of arms, but it provides a route for investigating how identifying signs were selected and understood.

The distinction between Rupenid and Hetumid dynastic contexts also needs to be retained when interpreting royal objects. The paired rulers on the coin discussed earlier belong to a particular political relationship. Calling it merely “a medieval Armenian coin” loses the opportunity to ask how rule and succession were being represented through the pair.

For a corpus, record royal coins alongside manuscripts commissioned by identified patrons and securely attributed seals. Ask which signs recur across media, which identify a dynasty and which perform another task within sacred narrative. A convincing continuity argument requires repeated, contextualized witnesses. Similar decoration on two isolated objects provides a question to investigate rather than a completed genealogy.

The institution's exhibition account is a modern synthesis. Its named workshops and political setting guide research, while the individual catalogue entries supply the objects' dates, materials and attributions. Preserve that source hierarchy. An exhibition overview should not be made to support an invented folio description that has never been inspected.

## A lion and an eagle can identify evangelists

A Gospel of John title leaf illuminated by Sargis, dated 1300–1310 in the Met's record, includes the symbols of all four evangelists: angel, lion, ox and eagle. Bird-shaped lettering also forms part of the opening text.[^am-john-leaf] This is a concrete Armenian object in which familiar heraldic animals have a different identifying function.

The lion here refers to Saint Mark within the Gospel programme, not automatically to a royal dynasty. The eagle refers to Saint John. Their placement within an incipit and headpiece connects them with sacred text. If cropped into individual silhouettes, the animals can look deceptively like charges detached from a coat. The surrounding page explains what they are doing.

Bird-shaped letters add another category. A bird form can function as part of writing, retaining a letter's place in a word while making it ornamental. Reading the image requires attention to the text, not merely identifying the species. A heraldic index that classifies every such letter as a bird charge would invent an armorial population the manuscript does not contain.

The title page therefore makes a useful control beside the lion-and-cross coin. Ask how each object identifies its subject, which inscriptions contribute and where the animal sits in the composition. The same animal can participate in royal representation or an evangelist's identity. Neither use needs to be dismissed in order to classify the other accurately.

This comparison also shows why “Christian symbol” is too broad a final description. It may be true of both objects while concealing the difference between a named Gospel author and a royal image. More precise classification makes the wider connection meaningful rather than vague.

## Yerevan uses a distinctive civic palette and composition

Yerevan's municipal account describes a backward-looking lion holding a sceptre, with crown, tree-of-life reference, an eternity sign and an Ararat image. It names apricot and bronze colours, credits Albert Sokhikyan and gives dates for changes to the prescribing description.[^am-yerevan-symbols] This is a substantial civic case alongside the national achievement.

The lion's head turned back is an identifying feature of attitude. It should not be normalized to face forward because that would match a more familiar lion charge. The sceptre, crown and breast sign belong to the described civic composition, and their relationships need to be preserved. A general category such as “lion rampant” cannot supply all that information.

The palette is equally instructive. Apricot and bronze are colour names in this municipal description. They should be retained and explained, rather than silently converted into an English textbook's Or and Gules. A practical reproduction needs the approved model or technical colour specification in addition to a translated word.

That does not mean every shade appearing in a civic design becomes a universally available heraldic tincture throughout Armenia. It establishes what this particular bearer prescribes. The chapter can show the local vocabulary and its application without inventing a national tincture rule from one municipal emblem.

The city's inscription belongs to the design's identity as well. Translate it for English readers in the caption, preserving the original letter forms in an image that claims to reproduce the prescribed emblem. A wordmark translated into English may be useful in a communications project, but it should be credited as an adaptation rather than presented as the unchanged official file.

## A collector coin places the civic arms beside other identities

The Central Bank's Yerevan anniversary issue, designed by Vardan Vardanyan, combines the city's arms, plan and a tree-of-life architectural ornament on one face. The other represents a fragment of the city founders' sculpture.[^am-yerevan-coin] Several identifying references coexist on one modern object.

The city plan is not a heraldic charge merely because it appears beside the arms. The sculpture fragment similarly represents a civic monument through numismatic imagery. The coin's complete design can coordinate these distinct categories to commemorate the city. Describing every element as part of the coat would confuse the coin composition with the emblem it includes.

The anniversary date also marks a modern commemorative act, not the manufacture date of every historical referent shown. A coin celebrating an ancient foundation remains a modern issued object. Its historical narrative can be examined through the issuer's description, while the underlying claim about the foundation needs the relevant archaeological and textual evidence.

For a paired study, compare the civic emblem on the municipal page with its representation on the coin. Identify what reduction and engraving preserve, and which surrounding images are new to the coin. This demonstrates adaptation across media without treating every difference as a new civic coat.

## Graduate investigation: distinguish a historical sign from a revival

Build a dossier with the medieval royal coin, Sargis's Gospel leaf, the modern national achievement and Yerevan's civic emblem. Identify date, bearer or represented figure, medium, inscription and the animal's role in each. Include the source's exact degree of certainty for attribution and identity.

Then write a comparison of the eagle and lion across the objects. Distinguish supporter, royal identifying figure, evangelist attribute and civic charge. Explain what evidence would be required to establish inheritance or a direct continuity of one device. The resemblance alone should not perform that argument.

Add the Yerevan collector coin as a final case of modern commemoration. Explain which image is the civic arms and which surrounding images belong to the coin's design. A successful result will show how Armenia's modern symbols organize a long history while allowing the medieval and sacred objects to retain their own functions.

## Workshop: which past does the source establish?

A fictional article claims that the current arms are an exact unchanged ancient achievement because the Constitution refers to historical kingdoms. Rewrite the argument using the modern constitutional record, presidential history and medieval coin as separate witnesses.

<details class="research-answer"><summary>Discuss historical representation</summary><p>The Constitution establishes the present state composition and its historical references. The presidential account establishes the modern design and legislative chronology it reports. The coin establishes a specific medieval representation of authority and a lion-with-cross figure. Demonstrating exact continuity requires additional dated witnesses; the modern reference to a kingdom does not provide them automatically.</p></details>

For graduate work, select one internal historical reference in the state arms and build a small corpus of securely dated earlier objects. Record the confidence of each attribution and test the proposed continuity through the actual forms. The result may establish transmission, selective revival or a modern reconstruction, each of which deserves a precise historical account.
