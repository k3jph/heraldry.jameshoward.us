## Three public mottos and two independence settings

The Ghanaian coat of arms carries the motto **“Freedom and Justice”**, described by the [Ghana Permanent Mission to the United Nations](https://www.ghanamissionun.org/ghana-flag/). **Nii Amon Kotei** designed the arms adopted in **1957**, the year of Ghana's independence. The motto belongs to the official national composition. It does not automatically explain the private meaning of every black star or eagle in Ghanaian armorial design.

Namibia adopted its national arms at independence in **1990**. Its motto is **“Unity, Liberty, Justice”**, and the current formal description appears in **Schedule 1 of the National Symbols of the Republic of Namibia Act 17 of 2018**, which replaced the 1990 legislation. The [Namibian Permanent Mission to the United Nations](https://www.un.int/namibia/namibia/symbols) gives the government's explanatory context. The fish eagle, two oryx, head-ring, Namib dune and Welwitschia make the achievement distinctive. A reading that supplies the motto but omits the act's date and actual named animals cannot fully explain its public identity.

Kenya's national achievement places **“Harambee”** on its motto scroll, a Swahili expression commonly glossed as pulling together. The arms were adopted with independence in **1963** and are described in the [National Flag, Emblems and Names Act](https://kenyalaw.org/kl/fileadmin/pdfdownloads/Acts/NationalFlagEmblems_Names.pdf). The cock with an axe, paired lions and crossed spears must be read alongside the named motto rather than reduced to a universal chart assigning the same meaning to all African supporters.

## African state heraldry needs named cases

African heraldry cannot be adequately represented by one South African chapter and a handful of national thumbnails. [Kenya](/traditions/kenya/) supplies a distinct grant-and-registration system. Ghana and Namibia add two public achievements in which familiar heraldic positions carry locally specific objects, species and political references.

The question is how those components work within a named institution’s account. An identifying staff, a desert plant and a traditional head-ring are not decorative substitutes for a supposedly normal European device. Each can perform a defined structural role while communicating a local reference. The record should explain both operations.

These two cases do not stand for every African tradition. They provide a source-led comparison from which further national, civic and family corpora can develop. A continent is a geographical frame for research, not one armorial jurisdiction.

## Ghana: authority appears in different quarters

Ghana’s United Nations mission explains the ceremonial sword and linguist’s staff through traditional authority, the castle through national government, and cacao and mining through economic resources.[^gh-mission] Different kinds of authority and activity are therefore presented in distinct positions within the shield.

Read the arrangement before assigning a single slogan to the whole design. A ceremonial object identifies a particular social role; a castle refers to another institutional setting; a crop and mine describe resources. Combining them makes a national account, but it does not turn each component into the same kind of symbol.

The term *okyeame*, retained in the mission’s explanation, deserves more than the loose translation “translator.” It identifies the linguist or spokesperson named in that setting. Preserve the local term and the source’s account while seeking fuller research into the represented office. A broad English label can make a specific authority disappear.

## The lion and black star add wider relationships

The mission attributes a Commonwealth reference to the central lion and an African political reference to the black-star element.[^gh-mission] These meanings are the institution’s explanatory account. They should be cited as such rather than presented as universal meanings of every lion or star in heraldry.

The compositional mechanism is useful. Internal divisions can present local institutions and resources while a central or upper figure supplies another layer of political relationship. A single achievement can therefore make several connected statements without every charge sharing one origin or function.

For a research project, distinguish the design’s prescribed content from the chronology of each explanation. A current mission page is a witness to the present official narrative. The original design process requires its own commissioning records and artist documentation. Present meaning and original intent can correspond, but that correspondence should be demonstrated.

## Namibia: a head-ring beneath the crest

Namibia’s 2018 gazette schedule specifies a traditional head-ring beneath the fish-eagle crest, oryx supporters and a compartment containing Welwitschia.[^na-gazette] The head-ring occupies a recognizable supporting position while giving that position a particular local form.

This is a structural comparison with Peter Irniq’s Canadian parka hood and other adaptations discussed in [Canada](/traditions/canada/). The useful question is which role is preserved and how its realization changes. A conventional diagram may show a helmet or wreath; a particular instrument can specify another arrangement.

Do not infer that every traditional head-ring in every context is heraldically a torse. The statute assigns a form within this achievement. The same physical or cultural object elsewhere may have another function. A reference work should explain the particular assignment rather than reclassifying an entire cultural category through one national design.

## Species and landscape have prescribed positions

The gazette’s description identifies the fish eagle, oryx and Welwitschia in distinct crest, supporter and compartment roles.[^na-gazette] Moving the eagle into the shield or the plant above it would change the composition, even if every named species remained somewhere in the image.

The Namibian mission explains the figures through attributes such as vision, courage and survival and distinguishes the arms from the national seal.[^na-mission] Those symbolic interpretations and documentary functions should remain separate. The plant’s survival narrative explains one attributed meaning; it does not establish who may use the public device as an identifying mark.

Botanical precision is also useful. A generic succulent icon might communicate a desert setting while failing to represent the named species. The design’s local specificity can be lost through well-intentioned simplification. Description, approved model and actual rendering need to be compared before replacing a distinctive plant with a convenient stock image.

## Public arms and seals identify different acts

The Namibian sources distinguish the public achievement from the national seal and its custody.[^na-gazette][^na-mission] The same core device can be adapted to an authenticating instrument without making every reproduction an act of state authentication.

A seal impression on a document needs date, issuer and attachment. An illustration of the achievement on a mission page needs image provenance and its role as explanatory material. A designed emblem’s political importance does not eliminate these ordinary documentary distinctions.

Further research should extend both cases into municipal and institutional records and examine participation in the design process. Local references in a national picture do not alone establish which communities could choose or refuse them. The [colonial and Indigenous chapter](/traditions/colonial-indigenous/) supplies a broader model for asking that question without inventing an answer from appearance.

## Ghana’s cross organizes a national account

The Ghanaian embassy in Ankara credits Amon Kotei with the arms and Theodosia Salome Okoh with the flag. Its description places a green cross edged in gold across the shield, separating the staff-and-sword, castle-and-sea, cacao and mine imagery. A gold lion occupies the cross’s center.[^gh-ankara-emblems] The two designs have related national references, but their creators and compositions should remain distinct.

The cross is an ordinary doing organizational work. Its arms establish four areas while its center supplies a position for the lion. We can distinguish that layout from quartering four inherited coats. A shield divided into four pictorial areas does not automatically narrate four ancestral armorial bearings. In this case the official explanation concerns authority, government and resources. Genealogical marshalling is one use of a divided shield; it is not the definition of every four-part composition.

The embassy calls the ordinary a St George’s cross. That label describes its straight, upright form in the account, while the same description makes its green tincture explicit. Readers familiar with a red English cross should let the specified color control their reconstruction. The vocabulary has traveled into a different composition. The presence of the term alone cannot establish English ownership of the device or tell us that the Ghanaian ordinary must be recolored red.

Its gold edging is also a compositional instruction. *Fimbriation* is a narrow contrasting boundary around a charge or ordinary. Here it helps separate the green cross from the surrounding areas; it should remain narrow enough for the cross to read as the main figure. If a redrawing broadens the gold until it looks like a second independent cross, it can change the hierarchy. The matter is not simply whether the right colors appear somewhere. Their proportions and relationships make the description legible.

The staff and ceremonial sword introduce objects of authority without needing a European crown to translate them into importance. A viewer unfamiliar with the office represented by the staff may need a contextual explanation, just as a viewer unfamiliar with a European ecclesiastical hat needs one. Familiarity is an attribute of the viewer, not an objective measure of how clearly a cultural object signifies within its own setting. The chapter should explain the local office rather than replacing the object with a supposedly universal badge of rank.

## One black star has several positions

The embassy describes a black star with gold edging above the shield, on a wreath of national colors; black stars also hang from the gold eagle supporters’ neck ribbons.[^gh-ankara-emblems] Related figures can occupy several positions within one achievement. The repeated outline does not make those positions redundant.

An upper star can establish a visual summit. A star suspended from a supporter’s ribbon attaches the figure to that supporter through an object resembling an insignia. Both can participate in the national account, while their local relationships differ. A component inventory that says only “three black stars” preserves the count but loses the architecture. We need to record which star stands above the shield and which two are pendant from the supporters.

The distinction becomes practical in simplified artwork. If a small rendering removes the neck ribbons and lets the stars float beside the eagles, it may preserve every listed figure while changing their connections. If it replaces the upper star and wreath with a star inside the shield, it changes a structural position. A careful simplification might omit details in a clearly labeled secondary graphic; it should not silently become the sole authoritative model of the full achievement.

There is a further comparison with the national flag. A familiar black star can connect separate national devices without making the flag a banner of the shield’s entire design. A banner of arms transfers the shield’s composition to a flag’s surface. Ghana’s national flag has its own arrangement. National visual identity can operate through related elements across several devices rather than through one design repeated intact on every support.

## Namibia’s flag-derived shield has a particular direction

The Namibian schedule specifies blue, red and green, silver boundaries and a diagonal organization described through *bend sinister*. Its gold sun has twelve triangular rays and a blue annulet.[^na-gazette] Read these as instructions about geometry before interpreting the colors. A diagonal running in the wrong direction does not become correct because its palette is accurate.

For the viewer of an upright shield, a bend sinister normally rises from the lower left toward the upper right. The terminology again follows the bearer’s perspective. A flag and a shield also have different outlines: a rectangular flag may stretch the diagonal across a wide field, while the shield tapers toward its point. Transfer of the design requires preserving its organizing relationship, not mechanically cropping an arbitrary rectangle until it fits.

The sun gives us a useful small-scale example. Twelve triangular rays are a specified count and form. A generic stock sun with sixteen wavy rays would preserve the broad idea of sunlight while losing the described charge. The blue annulet is a ring, not a license to fill the entire center with blue. That distinction can become difficult to see in a tiny thumbnail. A larger approved representation and the written description help resolve what the small image obscures.

The schedule’s wording is not a perfectly frictionless exercise in elementary English blazon: it describes the tiercing and also specifies the red diagonal. A reader should follow the complete text together with its official model rather than “correcting” the national device into a tidier imaginary coat. Text and graphic are explicitly paired in the statute. Where phrasing allows a question, the prescribed image is part of the evidence, not an embarrassing extra to discard.

## A head-ring can occupy the torse position

The schedule names the green head-ring as the *torse* and places six joined gold lozenges on it. The fish eagle above it has specified wings, feet and head direction; the description even relates its beak to the lozenges below.[^na-gazette] The relationship between components is unusually instructive. The crest does not merely contain the correct species somewhere above the shield.

A conventional torse is a twisted band associated with the junction of helm and crest. Here the national description assigns that structural category to a particular head-ring. We can compare the role without insisting that the physical forms are identical. The head-ring’s six lozenges also provide reference points for positioning the eagle. Decoration on one component becomes part of the spatial instruction for another.

Imagine a redrawing that turns the eagle to face forward because symmetrical wings look pleasing. That change could break the beak’s specified relationship to the head-ring even if the artist retains the right bird, six lozenges and green color. This is a stronger test than asking whether a drawing looks broadly Namibian. The description establishes a connected arrangement. Its fidelity is relational.

Do not add an unsupported ethnographic attribution to the head-ring. Calling it “traditional,” as the statute does, supplies one description; identifying a particular community, period or ritual use requires further evidence. We can explain the armorial position fully while leaving the more specific cultural history open. That distinction prevents a reference work from turning one official phrase into a confident invented story about an entire population.

## A desert plant supplies more than a setting

The Namibian compartment names a sand-dune, Welwitschia and three cones below the base.[^na-gazette] A botanical charge can therefore carry several requirements: the identity of the plant, its placement and specified reproductive structures. Treating the compartment as generic green ground would lose the named desert setting; substituting a convenient cactus would lose the plant.

The difference is analytically useful. A heraldic artist stylizes. Stylization can reduce surface detail while retaining diagnostic structure, just as a stylized lion keeps features that allow it to read as a lion. The question is which features preserve this plant’s identity and its prescribed arrangement. A botanical reference and the approved model are better starting points than a search for any icon tagged “desert.” Local specificity survives through disciplined simplification.

The oryx supporters make the same point at another scale. Supporters relate to the shield through their stance and placement; they also remain identifiable animals. If they are made into generic antelopes, the drawing can lose a biological reference even while retaining the familiar two-supporter frame. Structure and species must be read together. Neither the European provenance of some technical vocabulary nor the African provenance of the species can explain the full achievement alone.

## Production files belong to a use system

Namibia’s presidential asset collection separates national-seal, armorial and presidency-logo files.[^na-presidency-assets] This is useful evidence of how related official graphics are delivered for production. File separation helps a designer select a resource for a particular task, but the directory is not by itself a new act adopting each design.

There are several different transformations to track. A black-and-white rendering changes the reproduction method. A seal adaptation places identifying content into an authenticating device. A presidency logo can place the achievement within an office’s visual identity. These transformations can share components without having identical functions. A file’s creation or upload date also belongs to that particular digital witness, not necessarily to the first adoption of the national symbol.

A practical exercise makes the distinction clear. You are asked to illustrate an educational discussion of Namibia’s national arms, print an authenticating government seal and create an identity for an unrelated private organization. The same attractive SVG cannot answer all three requests merely because it is downloadable. Educational reproduction raises source and rights questions; an authenticating seal raises authority and custody; the private identity raises a different question about use of a public device. Determine the task before choosing the asset.

## Workshop: preserve position and attribution

Make component tables for the two achievements. Record position, object or species, source’s stated meaning and the evidence needed for original design intent. Then describe what would be lost if every local object were replaced by a generic European charge with a similar silhouette.

<details class="research-answer"><summary>Discuss the local forms</summary><p>The loss would concern named offices, species and cultural forms as well as visual detail. A heraldic position can remain familiar while its realization is locally specific. Current institutional symbolism should be attributed to the institution, while commissioning files are needed for claims about the original design process and participation.</p></details>

A graduate project can compare one adapted structural position across Ghana, Namibia, Kenya and Canada. Define the role before selecting images, then examine each instrument and explanation. The result should show how institutions authorize and document adaptation, rather than deciding that a design is less heraldic because its local references are unfamiliar to a European handbook.
