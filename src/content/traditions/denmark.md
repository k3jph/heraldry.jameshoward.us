## The simple shield and the larger achievement

Denmark’s state shield bears three crowned blue lions and nine red hearts on gold. Rigsarkivet’s 2025 guidance explains both the components and the possibility of redrawing the arms within heraldic principles.[^dk-guide] That simple shield needs to be distinguished from the larger royal achievement, whose internal arrangement has changed through dated decisions.

The distinction is practical. A visitor seeing three lions on a public office’s identity material is not necessarily seeing the whole royal achievement reduced to a small icon. The bearer, authorized use and graphic form matter. An account that labels every Danish state symbol “the royal crest” makes it harder to understand the actual administrative relationships.

Use three fields in your research record: the identifying design, the institution or person using it, and the version or rendering of the image. A stable design can appear through different drawings. A royal achievement can incorporate that design while also making a wider statement about dynastic and territorial relationships.

## Permission and approval are different acts

Rigsarkivet states that public authorities can use the relevant state symbols while the design of the crown and arms requires its heraldic guidance and approval. The institution has served as State Heraldic Consultant since 1985.[^dk-public] A right to use a device and approval of a particular rendering consequently answer different questions.

For a worked example, imagine a government agency commissioning a new identity system. The agency’s status may permit use of the crown. The designer still needs to know how that crown should be drawn and combined with the remaining material. Graphic approval ensures the rendering respects the public symbol; it does not create the agency or confer every possible use on unrelated commercial organizations.

This is a useful corrective to the idea that heraldry consists only of grants to families. Public administration includes advice, reproduction standards and protection against misleading use. A state archive can carry out those functions without operating as an English-style college for every private armorial claim.

## What a blazon permits an artist to change

The 2025 graphic guide treats the arms as a design identified through description rather than one immutable drawing.[^dk-guide] That gives the artist room to change line, proportion and stylistic treatment while preserving identifying relationships. It also places limits on simplification: the three lions and nine hearts still have jobs to do.

Suppose a new small-format drawing preserves the count, positions and tinctures while simplifying the lions’ fur. Compare that with a drawing omitting several hearts or turning the blue lions black. The first may be an artistic realization of the same design; the others alter specified content. The actual guide and approval process, rather than a viewer’s preference, determine acceptability for the intended official use.

A digitized database should therefore keep a canonical description separate from downloadable renderings. Otherwise each redesign becomes a supposedly new coat of arms, or every old image is overwritten by the newest official file. Both choices erase useful history. Versioned artwork permits researchers to study stylistic and administrative change without losing design identity.

## The royal revision announced in 2025

The Royal House announced a new royal achievement on 1 January 2025, established by resolution of 20 December 2024. The Faroe Islands and Greenland received separate fields; Schleswig was retained, while the three crowns were removed.[^dk-royal-2025] This is a current, documented revision, and a chapter using only the former 1972 arrangement would give readers an obsolete account.

The change supplies a concrete case of political emphasis through composition. Giving a device its own field changes its relationship to neighboring devices and its prominence in the whole shield. A technical description of the rearrangement and the institution’s explanation of its purpose should be recorded separately, allowing the reader to examine how the two correspond.

This does not replace the simple Danish state shield with a new national design of unrelated content. The royal composition incorporates several identifying levels. Preserve the distinction between Denmark, the wider Realm invoked by the notice and the monarch’s achievement. It is exactly the kind of distinction that a single thumbnail caption tends to suppress.

## Territorial figures need territorial histories

The Faroese ram and Greenlandic bear deserve research through their own institutions and objects. Their appearance in the royal composition establishes one relationship at one date. It does not exhaust Faroese or Greenlandic visual identity, nor turn every local emblem into a Danish family-armorial derivative.

A comparative project should follow a device in both local and royal settings. Ask which image was used by which body, under what instrument, and for what audience. The exercise can reveal a stable motif performing different political work. It can also identify where the historical accounts depend on a later summary that must lead back to seals, architectural objects or adoption records.

The [Iceland chapter](/traditions/iceland/) provides a nearby comparison in which constitutional change affects the surrounding achievement. [Norway](/traditions/norway/) demonstrates another separation between state and royal forms. The Nordic material is connected without being governed by one identical institutional system.

## Three lions and nine hearts are separate instructions

Denmark’s simple state shield specifies three crowned blue lions and nine red hearts on gold.[^dk-guide] The count of lions and the count of smaller figures both contribute to the design. A reduction that keeps three animals but replaces the surrounding figures with arbitrary red dots has lost information. The challenge is to make the smaller forms recognizable while preserving the larger animals’ room to function.

The lions also have a position and orientation. A viewer should be able to distinguish their ordered arrangement from three rampant animals scattered independently around the field. Their relationship gives the composition its rhythm. The crowns are attributes of the lions, while the hearts occupy the field. Putting the hearts on the animals would create another relationship even if the palette and total count were unchanged.

A controlled drawing exercise can isolate those requirements. Begin with the gold field and three evenly spaced blue lion silhouettes, then refine the posture, crowns, and surrounding hearts. Test the result at a large size and as a small printed shield. If detail vanishes, adjust the drawing rather than changing the number of specified figures. A faithful simplification retains the identity’s operations instead of solving the problem by replacing the arms.

The contrast relationships are also more precise than the overall palette suggests. Blue animals and red hearts each meet the gold field, while gold crowns meet blue lions. The immediate background determines the reading of each component. The [tincture lesson](/academy/tinctures/) develops that method; the Danish shield supplies a compact case in which several tinctures occupy different layers rather than one undifferentiated pattern.

## The royal cross organizes the quarters

The royal achievement uses a cross to organize territorial quarters and carries a smaller dynastic shield in the centre. The Royal House’s explanation identifies Denmark, the Faroes, Greenland, and Southern Jutland in the current arrangement, with the Oldenburg bars in the inescutcheon.[^dk-royal-composition] These devices have different roles. A territorial quarter and the dynastic centre should not be counted as interchangeable pieces of one family pedigree.

The cross is itself part of the composition. It separates and relates the fields, while its form affects the space available to their figures. A straight-sided cross and one with outwardly curved arms create different shapes at the edges of the quarters. The artist has to make the constituent devices legible within the prescribed structure rather than pasting four independent shield images into rectangular boxes.

The inescutcheon establishes another scale. Its bars occupy a small shield lying over the larger organization. The central placement gives the dynastic reference prominence despite its smaller area. Describing the bars as an extra fifth territorial quarter would miss that relationship. The visual grammar assigns meaning through position, enclosure, and overlap, not simply through the inventory of charges.

This is an instructive comparison with the [Swedish greater arms](/traditions/sweden/) and the [Dutch state and royal arms](/traditions/netherlands/). All join royal identity with wider public representation, but their actual compositions and instruments differ. The similarities help us formulate the questions; the local description supplies the arrangement we need to draw and explain.

## A new quarter can change emphasis without inventing a new device

The 2025 announcement discussed earlier changes the organization of the royal achievement. A constituent device can have an older history while receiving a new position or greater visual space in the revised composition. The Royal House’s account locates the Faroese ram in earlier seal and church-furnishing evidence and the Greenlandic bear in a seventeenth-century royal context.[^dk-royal-composition] The age of a figure and the age of its current placement are therefore different parts of the chronology.

That distinction is useful for interpreting emphasis. Giving an identity a separate quarter changes how the complete achievement presents its constituents. The figure need not change to acquire a different relationship with its neighbours. A study of the revision should compare position, field, scale, and surrounding structure alongside the institution’s stated rationale. Merely listing which animals appear misses the design’s political and compositional decisions.

An invented comparison can demonstrate the operation. Place a ram and bear together in one quarter, then give them separate quarters in another teaching composition. Keep both figures unchanged. The second version distributes the visual space differently and makes the relationship between the represented identities more explicit. The exercise is a model of composition, rather than an attributed reconstruction of an earlier royal drawing.

The revised achievement also has a date of determination and a date of public appearance. Their distinction lets us identify the governing act and the new realization without pretending that every object displaying an older version was instantly remade. Physical replacement has its own history. A surviving sign or seal with an earlier composition can continue to be valuable evidence of the display it actually records.

## Order collars and supporters complete the royal display

The greater display surrounds the shield with royal order collars, supporters, and a pavilion. The Royal House explains that a reduced rendering can omit supporters and pavilion in specified settings.[^dk-royal-composition] Those versions are related realizations whose included components differ. A shield-only reproduction can preserve its core composition while leaving out information about the complete ceremonial arrangement.

The surrounding parts need descriptions of their own. A collar encircling the shield is distinct from an order’s badge appearing on a ribbon elsewhere in an object. Supporters hold or flank the achievement rather than becoming charges in its quarters. The pavilion provides a larger ceremonial frame. Calling all of these “decorations” would conceal their different positions and identifying functions.

The medium then affects how the components are realized. A large ceremonial drawing can retain fine distinctions in collars and fabric. A seal has other constraints of size, relief, and lettering. A textile may require adaptation through stitching or weaving. The identity moves across those media by preserving specified relationships while the particular craft supplies the realization.

The comparison becomes historical when actual objects are located. Identify which version appears on a document or garment, what function that object performed, and when it was made. The complete achievement should be studied through its users and settings, rather than assumed to have one universally preferred amount of ornament.

## Copenhagen’s arms participated in a political bargain

Copenhagen City Archives connects the enlarged civic arms of 1661 with the city’s defence during the Swedish siege and the subsequent privileges granted by the king. Its account places the additions beside the citizens’ expectations of political influence.[^dk-copenhagen-1661] Heraldic change appears within a political relationship, not simply as a designer deciding that the old towers looked lonely.

A symbolic gift and a distribution of political authority are different outcomes. An enlarged achievement can publicly honour a community while the terms of its participation in government are settled elsewhere. The historian should therefore compare the armorial display with the privilege instrument and the political chronology. The image expresses a relationship; it does not by itself disclose every consequence of the bargain.

The older three-tower identity gives the enlarged composition a recognizable core. Added military and royal references connect that core with a particular account of service and patronage. This is the mechanism by which an augmentation can make history visible without abandoning the institution’s identifying form. A comparison of earlier and later witnesses should identify exactly which additions were made and where they sit in the achievement.

This case also helps explain why complexity needs to be evaluated through use. The enlarged display can carry a ceremonial story that a small mark could not show clearly. A simplified realization may preserve the core while reducing the surrounding narrative. The two can coexist as historically related forms whose purposes and evidentiary values differ.

## Three towers can identify a place or a producer

The city archive’s separate account follows commercial use of the towers and Copenhagen’s 1938 effort to protect and redraw its arms as a trademark.[^dk-copenhagen-commercial] The same recognizable vocabulary could associate goods with the city while creating concern about confusion with municipal identity. The historical problem concerns what the viewer is invited to infer from the mark.

On a product, a tower device can act as a manufacturer’s identifying sign or a reference to locality. On a municipal document, a related form can identify the public issuer. Those are different statements. A buyer may recognize Copenhagen without being meant to believe that the municipality made the object; an official-looking presentation can nevertheless blur the relationship. The surrounding words, medium, and use matter alongside the figure.

The redrawing adds a graphic layer to the protection history. A particular finished version can become the controlled model for an institutional purpose while older heraldic realizations remain part of the historical corpus. A fixed commercial or administrative drawing and a heraldic description can operate together. Their coexistence should be explained through the actual decision rather than a universal claim that arms either permit unlimited redrawing or must always use one outline.

For a study, compare a commercial tower mark, an older civic seal, and the later institutional rendering. Establish the bearer, date, and function of each. The result can explain how a familiar identifying form circulated across public and commercial settings, including where the records show an attempt to define or defend the boundary.

## Workshop: describe the layer that changed

Prepare a component inventory of the current royal achievement, separating quartered field, cross, inescutcheon, collars, supporters, and pavilion. Compare it with one dated earlier realization from a verified source. For each difference, say whether it concerns a constituent device, its position, or an external component. This produces a usable account of the revision instead of a before-and-after list of animals.

Then take Copenhagen’s towers as a civic case. Describe the recognizable core and one documented addition or adaptation. Explain what the object identifies and which institutional relationship its context supplies. Together, the exercises join formal analysis with the actual political and commercial work of Danish heraldry.

## Workshop: classify the institutional act

A fictional file contains an agency’s authorization to use state symbols, a heraldic consultant’s drawing approval and a royal resolution changing an achievement. Label the question answered by each. Then explain why the agency cannot use the royal revision as a grant of private arms to its director.

<details class="research-answer"><summary>Discuss the three acts</summary><p>Authorization concerns the user’s status and permitted use. Drawing approval concerns the rendering. The royal resolution concerns the defined royal design. None of those acts establishes a private family grant to an unrelated individual. Keeping act, bearer and design in separate fields prevents a public-symbol record from being converted into an unsupported personal entitlement.</p></details>

For graduate work, compare two official renderings made before and after the 2024 resolution. Produce a component inventory and classify each difference as content, arrangement or artistic treatment. Cite the adoption notice for the design change and the graphic guidance for rendering principles; do not require one document to supply both histories.
