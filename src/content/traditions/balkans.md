## A region is not one armorial system

The Balkans contain overlapping imperial, dynastic, religious and local histories. [Serbia](/traditions/serbia/), [Greece](/traditions/greece/), [Croatia](/traditions/croatia/), [Slovenia](/traditions/slovenia/) and [Romania](/traditions/romania/) have separate readings. This chapter adds a structured comparison of five further settings rather than treating them as interchangeable products of one regional influence.

The governing question is how a modern public device relates to the historical material invoked in its composition. A medieval-looking lion can enter a twentieth-century statute. A helmet associated with a hero can become a stylized charge. A landscape composition can retain an earlier structure after an ideological element is removed. Those are different mechanisms, and the adoption records let us distinguish them.

## Bulgaria: a crown within republican arms

The Bulgarian Assembly’s law excerpt, published in 1997, specifies a crowned gold lion, further lion supporters, a crown, oak branches and motto.[^bg-law] The composition retains historical monarchical reference within the identifying device of a republic. Its constitutional status cannot be determined by the crown alone.

The official Bulgarian wording should be compared with English accounts before normalizing technical terms. Translation can introduce awkward equivalents for posture and crown elements. Keep the original passage, editorial description and statutory graphic model separate, then explain the actual arrangement. That method protects students from memorizing a translation accident as a heraldic feature.

For a source-reading exercise, identify which lion belongs inside the shield and which lions support it. Their repeated species does not make their compositional roles identical. Count positions and relationships rather than merely counting animals in the whole image.

## Albania: a helmet with a collection history

The Albanian diplomatic account distinguishes the black double-headed eagle on red from the shield composition adding Skanderbeg’s gold helmet.[^al-symbols] The helmet is a specific historical reference, not a conventional rank crown placed above any aristocratic shield.

The Kunsthistorisches Museum records the surviving helmet and its appearance in the Ambras inventories of 1593 and 1596.[^al-helmet] This material history provides a defined witness to compare with the modern stylization. The museum object’s surviving construction, historical attribution and later emblematic use belong in separate records.

A modern gold silhouette can refer to an object whose materials and repairs are more complicated than the silhouette suggests. It need not reproduce every construction detail to communicate the reference. The researcher’s task is to identify the route of association, not to mistake a stylized charge for a conservation drawing.

## Montenegro: the eagle carries another device

Article 4 of Montenegro’s Constitution identifies a gold double-headed eagle with a lion on its chest.[^me-constitution] The eagle and the internal lion therefore perform related but distinguishable compositional work. Describing only a double-headed eagle loses the nested identifying content.

Compare the arrangement with Serbia and Albania without assigning all three devices one bearer. Shared figures can participate in distinct political accounts and legal instruments. The comparison should identify tinctures, internal shields or devices, surrounding features and named authority before proposing a historical transmission route.

The brief constitutional description is also distinct from detailed graphic and use rules. It tells the researcher what principal device is constitutionally identified. A complete account of permitted rendering and public use requires the further statutory witnesses relevant to those questions.

## Bosnia and Herzegovina: the adoption route matters

The High Representative’s decision of 18 May 1998 brought the coat-of-arms law into force on an interim basis. It describes a blue pointed shield, yellow triangle and white stars, with an incorporated graphic annex.[^ba-decision] The route by which the device became operative is central to its history.

An account concentrating only on medieval antecedents would miss this modern institutional act. The composition can be studied through its relationship to the flag, but that visual relationship does not explain the authority invoked to adopt it. Design, vote, decision and later legal development need separate dates and witnesses.

This case also demonstrates that a modern shield need not contain an ancient dynasty’s animal to function as arms. Heraldic study concerns identifying compositions and their institutions, including the political circumstances in which new forms are established.

## North Macedonia: change without a new shield

The Macedonian Heraldic Society’s original research follows proposals between 1992 and 2014 and identifies the 2009 removal of the red star while retaining the crop-and-landscape composition.[^mk-research] Proposal and adoption are distinct categories, and the historical publication’s country name should remain unchanged in its citation.

Removing one element can revise a design’s ideological presentation without replacing its whole structure. Students should inventory what remains as carefully as what disappears. A landscape framed by crops still performs a public identifying function even where its composition differs from the familiar shield-and-supporters diagram.

The [socialist emblems chapter](/traditions/socialist-emblems/) develops that comparative grammar. It investigates these forms through their own public-symbol institutions instead of treating them as failed attempts to draw Western family arms.

## Early modern armorials are another historical layer

Rudić’s research on the Illyrian Armorial connects its production with claims of noble descent and its political-cultural setting.[^rs-illyrian] Such a witness can illuminate early modern regional imagination without independently authenticating every medieval state or family attribution it contains.

When comparing an armorial image with modern national arms, draw the chain of witnesses. Did the modern designer cite that armorial? Are there independent earlier seals? Do later books merely copy the same manuscript tradition? The answers determine whether the connection is documented inheritance, deliberate revival or a resemblance still awaiting explanation.

## Sofia's arms began with a commission for an exhibition

Sofia's municipal history gives an unusually concrete design occasion: the 1900 Paris exhibition requested the city's arms for display. Haralampi Tachev's retrospective account describes selecting antique coin imagery, the church of St Sophia, Vitosha and a central lion, with a mural crown above.[^bg-sofia-arms] A modern civic coat assembled historical references for a contemporary international audience.

The commission date and the dates of the represented motifs must be separated. An antique coin can provide a figure for a coat created in 1900. It does not make the complete coat an ancient municipal achievement. The city's past becomes material for the designer's selection and arrangement, and the selection itself belongs to its modern history.

The small central shield is also important. A lion in an inescutcheon has a different relationship to the main composition from a lion added as a fifth equal quarter. Identify the outer shield's divisions before describing the central addition. The construction gives the lion a particular position within the civic argument.

The municipal chronology identifies later additions and revisions: motto and branches, a socialist star and stylization, and restoration of the earlier form. These dates show that “Sofia's arms” can refer to several historical versions. An object with a star should be dated through that sequence, not automatically corrected to the currently displayed image.

The city's English account repeats a lower-field direction in its narrative description. That is a translation or editorial issue to check against the approved image, rather than a reason to place two different motifs in one quarter and leave the other empty. The source is valuable because it preserves the designer's reasoning; precise reproduction still requires a coherent model.

This civic case belongs beside Bulgaria's national lion. The city does not merely use the state arms under a different name. Its composition brings local history, geography and an asserted connection with an earlier capital into a distinct bearer. A regional tradition becomes clearer when national and civic uses are allowed to differ.

## Bulgaria's achievement coordinates three lions and an external crown

The Bulgarian national law describes a crowned gold lion on a red shield, with two crowned gold lion supporters, a crown above and an oak-branch compartment with a motto.[^bg-law] The central lion and the supporters are three separate components. Their resemblance does not put all three inside the shield.

Count at the appropriate level. One lion belongs to the shield's content; two support the shield externally. A shield-only rendering can identify the central coat while omitting the full achievement. A complete official rendering must preserve the external arrangement prescribed for it. A caption should state which format the reader is seeing.

The crowns likewise occupy different positions. The charge and supporters are crowned, and the achievement carries an external crown. A direction to “remove the crown” would therefore be ambiguous unless it specifies which one. The law's composition should be mapped component by component before any discussion of republican use or historical association.

The oak branches also differ from a generic laurel wreath. A compartment gives the supporters a common base and can carry its own local or historical significance. The entire composition is not simply a red rectangle with a lion pasted on it. External elements coordinate the complete public representation.

For historical interpretation, use the law to establish the contemporary design and dated objects to investigate earlier lion forms. An old seal's lion can be related to the modern national motif without proving every present crown and branch existed in the same arrangement. The history of a charge and the history of a full achievement require different scales of evidence.

## Albania's helmet is both a surviving object and an emblematic reference

The Kunsthistorisches Museum's construction account identifies the Skanderbeg helmet as a late-fifteenth-century Italian sallet fragment, with a copper band and a gilt-copper goat head. It gives armoury accession A 127 and connects the object with Ambras inventories.[^al-helmet-construction] These are material and collection facts, distinct from the simplified gold helmet in Albania's state-symbol description.

The goat head provides a crest-like three-dimensional feature on an actual helmet. Its appearance on the historical object does not mean that every drawn representation must reproduce its copper construction in naturalistic detail. A national emblem can stylize the helmet into a recognizable sign. The physical artefact and its graphic representation should each be described according to their own medium.

The sallet fragment also reminds us that objects can survive through alteration or incomplete preservation. A modern emblem can present a complete-looking helmet while the museum record describes the material actually retained. The graphic should not be used to reconstruct missing metal without comparison with the physical evidence.

The late inventories establish that the object was associated with Skanderbeg in that collection at their dates. They do not independently prove every proposed episode in which he wore it. A secure collection-history statement and a heroic biography need not have the same degree of certainty. Preserve the museum's dating and qualifications when moving from object to national reference.

Compare the present flag's eagle with the state's helmet-bearing shield. Both use the black double-headed eagle on red, but their formats and added elements differ.[^al-symbols] A flag image is not a complete substitute for the coat, and a coat on a red rectangle does not automatically reproduce the flag's exact specification.

An exhibition could therefore show the museum object's record, a dated state-symbol model and a national flag in three separate panels. The comparison explains how a remembered hero and a recognizable eagle enter related public designs. It is more precise than calling all three “Skanderbeg's arms” and losing the distinctions between person, object and modern state.

## Montenegro's gold eagle differs from neighbouring white and black ones

Montenegro's constitutional description identifies a gold double-headed eagle carrying a lion on its chest. Serbia's public device uses a white eagle, while Albania's flag uses black on red.[^me-constitution][^al-symbols] The charge family is shared for comparison, but tincture and the breast device distinguish the compositions.

A small silhouette can erase those differences. Once the field, colour and chest sign disappear, “a double-headed eagle” becomes a broad label rather than a secure identification. Restore the full device before assigning a bearer. This is especially important for monochrome souvenirs or damaged architectural displays.

The lion on the chest needs its own internal arrangement. It is not a third head of the eagle or an unrelated supporter placed below. A breast device can represent another identifying layer within the eagle-bearing achievement. Compare its shield, field and ground with the approved model when those details are at issue; the constitutional summary alone may not define every graphic feature.

The red national flag with its central arms and gold edge supplies another distinct format. Its rectangular field surrounds the achievement; it should not be confused with the field of an internal shield. A rendering that transfers the flag's border onto the lion's shield has crossed between two levels of the design.

The date and political setting of adoption also belong in the record. A later constitution can retain an emblem already selected under an earlier act. The constitutional date is therefore not necessarily the first use date of the model. To trace the complete sequence, collect the adoption law, standards and subsequent constitutional reference separately.

## Bosnia's medieval textiles need their own identification argument

The National Museum of Bosnia and Herzegovina describes fifteenth-century purple brocade recovered at Kopošići in 2015 and attributed to Mirko Radojević through the archaeological and kinship investigation. The fabric carries figures described as lions or griffins and is treated as probably imported.[^ba-brocade-mantle] It is a significant medieval identity and status object, but the pattern is not automatically a proven hereditary coat.

The lion-or-griffin wording is a useful qualification. If the fragment does not securely establish the creature, do not improve the caption by choosing the more heraldically satisfying animal. Describe the surviving body, wings or other features and retain the ambiguity. Better photography or textile comparison may refine the identification; confidence alone cannot restore a missing section.

Repeated creatures on a costly fabric can communicate status, fashion or affiliation without identifying one family through a unique shield. To argue that they are personal arms, seek a matching securely attributed seal, a shield arrangement or repeated family use. The cloth's association with a named burial gives an owner context; it does not settle the symbolic function of every woven motif.

The textile also differs from the modern state's star-and-triangle shield. Their relationship is geographic and historical, not a direct visual identity. The modern adoption decision documents a particular postwar public symbol, while the textile documents elite material culture centuries earlier.[^ba-decision] A national chapter can contain both without pretending they are successive emblazonments of one unchanged coat.

The modern decision's institutional route remains essential. Who adopted the model and under which authority is part of what the emblem means politically. Reducing the act to a designer's colour choice would conceal that process. Conversely, knowing the enactment route does not identify the uncertain medieval creature on the textile. Each source has a defined job.

## North Macedonia distinguishes proposals from adopted emblems

The national-emblem research introduced earlier distinguishes proposed shield forms from the adopted landscape-and-crop composition and the 2009 removal of its red star.[^mk-research] A widely circulated lion proposal does not become the current state coat through repetition on heraldic websites.

Record a proposal's sponsor, date, drawing and procedural outcome. If the proposal remained unadopted, retain that status in its caption. It can still illuminate debate about historical identity and preferred heraldic form. A rejected or pending design is evidence of an argument, not evidence that the argument prevailed.

The removal of a star is a different scale of change from replacing the entire emblem with a shield. The landscape and surrounding agricultural forms can remain while one politically charged feature disappears. Such a revision should be analyzed in its actual extent. Calling it a complete restoration of medieval arms would describe a change that did not occur.

The chapter's [socialist-emblems comparison](/traditions/socialist-emblems/) supplies a wider vocabulary for wreaths, landscapes and stars. A public device need not conform to a shield-centred English beginner's model in order to have a legal identity and a substantial visual history. Classification should explain its form rather than treat every non-shield emblem as a failed drawing exercise.

## Skopje's bridge and mountain expose a design constraint

A Macedonian Heraldry Society design account describes work on Skopje's arms under a requirement to retain the city charter's shield shape and listed motifs: Stone Bridge, Vardar, fortress and snow-covered mountain. The account notes that the description leaves colours and relationships underdetermined.[^mk-skopje-design-study] That is a concrete design problem within a prescribed civic identity.

The constraint divides the task into fixed content and unresolved rendering. The designer must preserve the named motifs and form, while deciding how to make them legible and coordinated. A design may improve the presentation without requiring an entirely new identifying vocabulary. Whether it becomes the city's official model is a further procedural question.

An underdetermined description also sets limits on reconstruction. Two artists can draw different compositions that include the listed features. To select the authoritative rendering, locate the approved image or implementing decision. The fact that a proposed version satisfies the words does not prove it was adopted.

The society's own territorial-heraldic presentation should therefore be identified as its presentation unless municipal adoption is separately established. Professional expertise can produce a persuasive model without automatically acquiring the government's authority to prescribe it. The distinction respects both the design work and the civic act.

For a practical exercise, draw two plausible arrangements from the listed motifs, then compare each with the documented official or proposed image. Mark the choices the words determined and the choices left open. The exercise demonstrates why a blazon intended for repeatable drawing must specify relationships as well as inventory.

## Graduate investigation: map the routes to adoption

Build a comparative table for Sofia, Albania, Montenegro, Bosnia and Herzegovina, and North Macedonia. Record bearer, current device, act or model date, earlier reference, adoption authority and one material witness. Distinguish the designer's proposal, an institutional historical explanation and a final prescribing act in separate columns.

Use Sofia's civic history, the Skanderbeg helmet and the Bosnian brocade as three object-based cases. Explain what each can establish about identity and what remains outside its evidence. The first documents a modern selection of historic motifs; the second combines material construction with later heroic association; the third requires a careful distinction between owner context and armorial function.

Finally, compare Skopje's underdetermined description with a national legal model that specifies colours and placement. Explain which additional documents a renderer needs in each case. The result should show several regional routes to public identity and heraldic use, rather than discovering that five countries all possess birds, lions and complicated histories.

## Workshop: classify the change

Compare Bulgaria’s law, Albania’s helmet reference, Montenegro’s nested device, Bosnia and Herzegovina’s adoption decision and North Macedonia’s revision. Identify one distinctive evidential problem in each. Then explain why a single category called “Byzantine-style arms” would conceal more than it reveals.

<details class="research-answer"><summary>Discuss the five cases</summary><p>The cases respectively require reading republican historical reference, object attribution and stylization, nested composition, the authority and timing of adoption, and selective revision of an inherited emblem structure. Shared lions, eagles or regional histories cannot answer those different questions. Each needs its own instrument, bearer and chronology.</p></details>

A graduate project should follow one figure through three jurisdictions using only dated witnesses. Keep the evidence of direct borrowing separate from the interpretation of resemblance. A defensible comparison can identify a shared visual resource while still concluding that the transmission route remains unresolved.
