## Argument map

| Reading | Governing argument | Evidence and complication | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visual language | Identity persists through controlled variation, within social institutions | Dering witnesses, Zürich roll, messenger badge; resemblance is not descent | Compare witnesses, separate object and design dates, test identification |
| Anatomy | Components are relationships and institutional claims, not an accessory collection | Canadian records, Garter installations, Prankh helm, Swedish statute | Reconstruct component roles and distinguish omitted from absent |
| Geometry | A relational coordinate system survives changes in support | Divisions, repetitions, lines, mirroring, seals; contour and optical balance | Draw alternatives, diagnose transformations, record damaged geometry |
| Ordinaries | Geometry becomes named figures whose meaning depends on topology and scope | Width, diminutives, edges, intersections, charged ordinaries; classification disagreement | Reconstruct layer graphs and defend proportion choices |
| Charges | A figure's identity is a bundle of meaningful features | Attitudes, attributes, bird conventions, objects, plants, hybrids, canting | Compare diagnostic features and explain uncertain identifications |
| Blazon | Compact grammar depends on shared conventions and a traceable witness | Scope, reference, nested syntax, source-language and parser limits | Parse, redraw, translate with apparatus, repair an ambiguous draft |
| Cadency | Familial relationship and armorial transmission are connected by institutions | England, Scotland, Canada; visual difference alone cannot reconstruct genealogy | Build an evidence-supported transmission graph |
| Marshalling | A composite display records a relationship or claim, not proof of it | Marriage, office, dynasty, Spain, Sweden, historical enamel | Map nested components, distinguish assertions from entitlement |
| Jurisdictions | Authority is a historically situated process with specific actors and acts | UK distinctions, Canada, Flanders, Ireland, Netherlands, Czechia, Sweden, South Africa; source genres differ | Follow procedure, compare dated original texts, identify research limits |
| Public life | A public emblem links authority, representation, production, and reception | Great Seal, Universities Canada, Garter, municipal reorganization, South Africa | Follow one emblem across media and institutional changes |
| Evidence workshop | A defensible conclusion needs a reproducible chain from witness to claim | Existing Getty and Met witnesses; dating, dependency, corpus selection, rights | Extended documentary/design capstones, staged audits and model discussions |

Each section must advance the argument through explanation, a worked case, a complication, or a consequence. Constructed examples are labeled. Institutional facts are attributed; institutional guidance is not substituted for an inspected case file. Original English syntheses are distinguished from transcriptions and translations. Human specialist review remains pending.

## Evidence and illustrations

Source notes retain genre, institution, dated act or source version, and the distinction between attributed information and independent analysis. The comparison includes English and Scottish transmission, Canadian grants and confirmations, Irish institutional succession, Flemish decrees, Dutch public-body procedure, Swedish statutes, Finnish archive history, Czech municipal principles, South African legislation, German public-emblem protection, the Italian constitutional provision, and French administrative witnesses. It is a source-based comparative foundation, not an exhaustive statement of every jurisdiction's law.

Eight original SVG assets provide desktop and mobile versions of four structural comparisons. They are generated by `scripts/generate-academy-diagrams.py`, contain fictional teaching designs, and claim no real entitlement. Neutral gray in the ordinary diagram denotes structure rather than a proposed armorial palette. Existing rights-documented Met enamel and chapel photographs are reused with their credits, object identifiers, image provenance, and qualified interpretation. No new protected register artwork is copied.
