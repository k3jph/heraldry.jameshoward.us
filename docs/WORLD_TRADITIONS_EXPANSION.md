# World Traditions expansion — 11 October 2026

For the subsequent 31-chapter expansion, see [World Traditions coverage](WORLD_TRADITIONS_COVERAGE.md). Counts below describe this earlier milestone.

## Delivered scope

Eighteen new teaching chapters bring World Traditions to 39 chapters. England, Wales, Ireland, and Scotland have separate readings. Canada, the United States, Brazil, and Malta receive institutional and object-based treatments. Ecclesiastical heraldry groups Catholic and Anglican chapters; Other Traditions groups the Sovereign Military Order of Malta and micronations. The comparative expansion covers Mamluk blazons, Ottoman tughras, Chinese/Korean rank badges, Chinese seals and collectors’ marks, Akan/Asante adinkra, and European house/merchant marks. Existing tamgas and kamon chapters remain in the collection.

## Evidence and reproduction

The source atlas now contains 122 dossiers, an increase of 78. New dossiers retain editorial synthesis and independent-specialist-review-pending status. English is recorded when the consulted catalogue is English even if its subject is Chinese, Korean, Arabic, or Ottoman. Latin and Danish labels are added to the language selector; newly consulted Portuguese, Italian, Swedish, Latin, and Danish titles have separate English renderings. Indexed catalogue access and live access failures are identified in relevant chapters. No fresh manuscript transcription or physical-object authentication is claimed.

Five added Met photographs were individually checked against Public Domain object metadata and the museum’s CC0 Open Access policy: 91.1.601, 38.149.2, 36.65.11, 53.60.15, and 58.64.16. All are unchanged museum web-large files. Local paths, original image URLs, dimensions, accession references, checksums, and rights evidence are recorded in `src/data/tradition-image-rights.json`. The bamboo-seal photograph shows the body rather than the printing face. The Korean badge photograph is the single-crane accession, distinguished from a group-level explanation of double-crane examples. No Smithsonian or British Museum image was reproduced without separately verified permission.

## Editorial boundaries

This is a substantial first teaching expansion, not a complete survey or a specialist-reviewed reference edition. The regional backlog remains open for original-language regulations, archival files, wider scholarship, conservation/provenance investigation, and review. Canadian Buff is discussed through a particular badge and a labelled color-role inference, not asserted as a universally classified metal. Catholic insignia use dated 1967 and 1969 witnesses rather than a timeless schedule. Irish application practice is dated to the consulted notice. Micronational claims remain attributed to their issuers; the collection makes no numerical claim about micronations’ share of modern heraldry.
