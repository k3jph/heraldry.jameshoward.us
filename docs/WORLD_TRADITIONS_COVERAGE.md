# World Traditions coverage expansion — 11 October 2026

## Delivered scope

This follow-up adds 31 independently editable teaching chapters and 31,734 words of chapter prose. World Traditions now has 70 chapters, and the source atlas has 223 dossiers, including 101 new records. The chapters use official instruments, registers, museum and archival catalogues, and attributed research arguments. Each has a contents navigation, source-linked footnotes, a worked problem with an answer discussion, and a graduate research direction.

Individual chapters cover Byzantium, Greece, Serbia, Ukraine, Russia, Denmark, Norway, Iceland, Switzerland, Austria, Estonia, Belgium, Luxembourg, Slovakia, Georgia, Armenia, Australia, New Zealand, Mexico, the Philippines and Kenya. Regional chapters cover additional Balkan traditions, Latin America and the Caribbean, and Ghana and Namibia. India follows colonial banners and the national-emblem adaptation. Cross-border chapters address Orthodox and Lutheran heraldry, Jewish personal and communal armory, the Teutonic Order, colonial and Indigenous participation, and socialist state emblems.

The existing Catholic, Anglican, SMOM and micronational readings retain their established groups. Jewish armory and the Teutonic Order join Other Traditions. Socialist emblems join Comparative emblem systems. The index describes the broader coverage, while its counts remain computed from the content. The atlas adds human-readable language labels for Albanian, Bulgarian, Estonian, Greek, Icelandic, Norwegian (Bokmål), Slovak and Ukrainian; all 30 consulted source languages have labels.

## Evidence boundaries

- Byzantium distinguishes an attributed scholarly argument about pre-1204 shields from proof of an empire-wide heraldic system. The Met embroidery’s revised liturgical function remains visible.
- Ukraine has its own chapter. Princely signs, Cossack devices, Volhynian jurisdictional changes and the constitutional provisions for state arms have separate chronologies.
- Denmark distinguishes the royal arms revised in 2025 from the simpler state arms. Austrian abolition and public-emblem statutes are dated witnesses, not personal legal advice.
- Kenya receives a national chapter because its statutory College of Arms has local grant and registration functions. Historical forms and more recent fee schedules are not presented as one current price list.
- Georgia retains the consulted English legal translation’s color term without silently turning it into a settled tincture classification. Armenia’s modern historical references are not treated as unchanged ancient arms.
- Peru’s 2025 statute is distinguished from an older official atlas. Consulted Mexican and Philippine statutory witnesses are dated rather than assumed to include every later amendment.
- Jewish communal flag-origin accounts and the consulted specialist abstract remain in disagreement. Teutonic historical privileges reported by the Order remain attributed accounts where the underlying instruments were not inspected.
- Colonial and Indigenous participation distinguishes identified bearer choices, creative roles and state representation from unestablished consultation. Indigenous imagery is not automatically classified as European heraldry outside its adopted context.

All new dossiers remain editorial syntheses awaiting independent specialist review. An English catalogue is recorded as English even when it concerns a non-English tradition. Indexed records and abstracts are identified where they delimit the consulted evidence. The collection does not claim fresh physical examination, a new critical edition, exhaustive country coverage, or complete legal consolidation.

## Reproduction
