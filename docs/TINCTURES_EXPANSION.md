# Tinctures chapter expansion

Date: 2026-10-10. Editorial teaching draft; specialist review remains open.

## Scope and argument

The existing Academy Unit 3 is expanded from 1,717 to 8,858 words of prose, measured by `chapterWordCount` (source-note definitions excluded). There are still twelve Academy units. The guided lecture and three-question introductory check retain their separate purpose.

The chapter proceeds through these reasoning moves:

1. Establish the difference between an observed appearance, a specified tincture, and an actual material.
2. Introduce the principal palette and separate armorial identity from an artist's realization or a manufacturing target.
3. Trace immediate layers before applying a contrast convention; distinguish adjacency, fimbriation, counterchanging, and marshalling.
4. Separate conventional contrast from the legibility of a particular output.
5. Follow Buff through military/material contexts and dated Army/Canadian descriptions; expose the limits of a global binary category.
6. Use Copper to distinguish a tincture, an object name, and an attributed symbolic account.
7. Examine deliberately separate terms in expanded Canadian palettes and the South African registration vocabulary.
8. Treat stains and rarity as historically situated claims rather than automatic biographies or unsupported frequency estimates.
9. Preserve patterns, scope of proper, and the distinction between monochrome observation and color interpretation.
10. Apply the distinctions in five documentary exercises with developed answers and a corpus research project.

Substantial paragraphs develop premises, examples, complications, and consequences. The Howard voice skill and previously inspected author samples guide that structure. No personal experiences, invented quotations, or unverified bearer identifications are added.
