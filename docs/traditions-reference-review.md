# World Traditions expansion: editorial and release review

Reviewed 11 October 2026. All 70 existing chapters now have substantial additions: 129,460 added prose words, excluding contents navigation and footnote definitions. The source atlas contains 552 records, 329 added in this change, across 34 source languages. The progress manifest records each chapter's before/after counts and added sections.

The additions develop historical objects, technical composition, institutional acts, worked readings and research exercises. National arms are distinguished from personal, corporate and religious bearers. Comparative systems retain their own vocabulary and practices. Statements based only on catalogue descriptions, abstracts or institutional interpretations retain those limits.

The editorial pass covered all chapter structures and the cited additions, with targeted checks of dates, attribution, terminology and source metadata. It is an internal editorial review, not independent specialist verification of every historical proposition. The chapters and atlas retain their specialist-review status; unresolved inscription readings and manuscript collation remain identified as further research.

Corrections and integration work included:

- Moving discussion ahead of exercises and source gateways, including Japanese, Portuguese, Baltic, central European and comparative chapters; regenerating every contents list afterward.
- Revising research notes that described subjects now covered in the same chapter.
- Correcting the author of the Kossuth-arms study to Zoltán Máté Albert and crediting both Stefano Carboni and Qamar Adamjee for the glass-technique essay.
- Correcting Steven Lewis Point's register locator to volume V, page 428.
- Replacing an unavailable Vatican PDF link with an accessible official account, explicitly separating its 2026 publication from the 2025 vacancy issues it describes.
- Keeping historical legal versions, current operational notices, proposals and completed acts distinguishable. Current Irish and South African administrative limitations are dated observations.
- Preserving genuine source-language labels, including Chinese, Catalan, Bosnian and Afrikaans. These labels describe consulted records, not every language historically spoken in a region.
- Disambiguating short source titles with their institution or catalogue identifier.

The object gallery adds Stowe House's 1806 armorial ceiling, the Bayeux embroidery, an 1827 Sanbaso nobori and Hiroshige's 1857 Sumiyoshi festival print. These bring the gallery to 39 studies; the homepage continues to feature three. Object records distinguish surviving textiles, modern reproductions and depicted banners, and carry image provenance and rights records.
